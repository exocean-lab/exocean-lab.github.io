Services & Instruments

Analyses and experiments we run for partners, and the equipment behind them.

How it worksWhat we offerOur instrumentsPlanning a visitOther CEREGE platforms

Beyond our own research, we run measurements and experiments for academic and private partners — within a collaboration, or as a paid service. You can send us your samples, or come and work with us in the laboratory; visitors pay a small lab occupation fee.

exocean is part of CEREGE's network of technical platforms, so a stay with us can be combined with analyses on other CEREGE platforms — stable isotopes, elemental chemistry, X-ray imaging, micropalaeontology and more.

How it works

  1. Write to us

    Tell us about your samples — type, number, volume — and what you need, at . If you would also like to use other CEREGE platforms, say so.

  2. We reply

    We tell you what is feasible, how to collect, preserve and send your samples, and how long it will take — with a quote for paid services.

  3. Samples or a visit

    Send us your samples, or come and run the work with us in the laboratory. Visitors pay a small lab occupation fee.

  4. Results

    You receive your data with a description of the methods and quality control. If your project is confidential, we agree on the conditions before you send samples.

What we offer

Water and sediment chemistry

  • Seawater carbonate chemistry

    Total alkalinity by potentiometric titration on samples down to 4 mL, and pH by spectrophotometry with purified indicator dye. Alkalinity is calibrated with Dickson certified reference materials.

    • Metrohm OMNIS titrator
    • Agilent Cary 60

  • Dissolved silicate and phosphate

    Silicate and phosphate concentrations in seawater, by colorimetry on a UV-Vis spectrophotometer.

    • Agilent Cary 60

  • Microsensor profiling

    Microprofiles of oxygen, pH, nitric oxide (NO) and hydrogen sulfide through sediments and other confined environments — in the lab, on ships right after sediment collection, or at coastal sites.

    • Unisense microsensors

Experiments and incubations

  • Sediment–water interface experiments

    Sediment incubations in six temperature-controlled reactors that reproduce benthic hydrodynamics, from calm abyssal plains to reefs. Open or closed to the atmosphere, in free drift or flow-through, with chemistry followed in the porewaters and the overlying water.

    • Rotating disk reactors
    • Julabo CORIO circulator

  • High-pressure incubations

    Water or solid samples held at up to 500 bar (roughly the pressure at 5,000 m depth) in a 600 mL vessel, with water sampling during the run. Gas composition cannot be measured with the current setup.

    • Büchiglas Novoclave

  • Cultures and incubations

    Culture of plankton, microbes and other small organisms, and incubation experiments, under controlled temperature and light — from 0 to above 35 °C in the incubators of our COREFF foraminifera facility. Light intensity and spectrum are measured with a PAR sensor.

    COREFF, our COntinuous REproduction for Foraminifera Facility, can host thousands of planktonic foraminifera at a time — mostly Mediterranean species, kept alive and reproducing with protocols we keep refining — alongside diatom strains grown as food for their different life stages. For live specimens, visits or the culture season, contact Julie Meilland ().

    • Friocell & Memmert incubators
    • PAR sensor

Organisms and samples

  • Respiration of single organisms

    Respiration of microbes, single foraminifera or other small plankton, measured with microsensors in very small volumes of water under a stereomicroscope.

    • Unisense NanoRespiration
    • Zeiss Stemi 508

  • Microscopy and imaging

    Observation and imaging of living plankton and other immersed samples on an inverted microscope, and of small samples under a stereomicroscope.

    • Zeiss Axiovert 5
    • Zeiss Stemi 508

  • Plasma cleaning of samples

    Removal of organic matter from samples by plasma cleaning.

    • Henniker HPT-100

Planning a visit

The laboratory is at CEREGE, on the Arbois plateau near Aix-en-Provence — see how to get here. Tell us your dates early: instruments are shared between projects, and experiments can run for days or weeks.

A stay with us can be combined with analyses on other CEREGE platforms; each has its own access conditions.

exocean within CEREGE

CEREGE groups its analytical and experimental facilities by area of expertise into six technical centres, each co-led by a technical and a scientific manager. exocean is one of the four laboratories of the Experimental Biophysics and Chemistry centre (technical manager Pierre Boutet, scientific manager Julie Meilland), together with the CIRENE technology hall, the colloid characterisation laboratory and the geomicrobiology laboratory.

Combine your visit with other CEREGE platforms

The platforms most often useful to our partners. Each one has its own access conditions and rates — we can put you in touch.

  • Stable isotopes · PANISS

    δ¹⁸O and δ¹³C of carbonates, clumped isotopes (Δ47, Δ48), δ¹⁸O, δ¹⁷O and δD of water, and δ¹⁵N, δ¹³C and δD of organic compounds. Open to academic collaborations and services.

  • Elemental chemistry · LA-ICP-MS

    About 90% of the periodic table in solution by ICP-OES or ICP-MS, and laser ablation of solids for semi-quantitative elemental maps. Open to public and private users.

  • Radiocarbon, biomarkers and sediment geochemistry · LGO2i

    Radiocarbon dating on the AixMICADAS accelerator mass spectrometer, organic compounds as environmental indicators, and the elemental composition of marine and lake sediment archives.

  • 2D and 3D X-ray imaging · MATRIX

    Micro- and nano-tomography, X-ray micro-diffraction and micro-XRF mapping of the same sample, all non-destructive. Open to all.

  • Automated micropalaeontology · MANTA

    Automated microscopes with AI recognition of coccolithophores, foraminifera, radiolarians, diatoms and pollen, and a robot that picks foraminifera for chemical analyses.

  • Microfossil extraction · LPM

    Extraction of microfossils and other bio-indicators from sediments, soils, plants and water samples, ready for microscopy or geochemistry.

  • Sedimentology · SEDIM

    High-resolution imaging with mosaics, grain-size and particle-shape distributions (with or without chemical pre-treatment), and identification of micro- and macro-remains such as shells and ostracods.
