Research

We follow carbon from the plankton that build shells at the sea surface to the seafloor where those shells dissolve or are buried — and read past climates in the sediments they leave behind.

From surface to seafloor

Our projects follow carbon from the plankton that build shells in the sunlit surface ocean, down through the water column to the deep seafloor, where those shells dissolve or are buried — and into the sediments that record past climates.

  1. 1MANGO — Mangrove lagoons and coastal oxygen, Senegal
  2. 2DYNAMITE — Aragonite shells of planktonic snails
  3. 3ASPERGE — Aragonite reaching the seafloor
  4. 4Deep-C — Carbonate dissolution at up to 1,000 bar
  5. 5ForCry — Past CO₂ from foraminifera in sediment cores
  • Sunlit surfaceplankton build carbonate shells
  • Water columnshells and organic matter sink
  • Deep seaup to 1,000 bar in the trenches
  • Seafloorshells dissolve or are buried
  • Sediment archivemillions of years of climate history

Our key questions, from the surface to the deep sea

  1. What controls spatial variability in oceanic carbon fluxes?
  2. How are carbon fluxes impacted by calcifying plankton population dynamics?
  3. What is the future of ocean biogenic carbon production and export?
  4. How does climate sensitivity depend on the background climate state?
  5. How do deep-sea processes mitigate anthropogenic CO₂?
  6. And how are human activities already leaving traces in the sediment record?
